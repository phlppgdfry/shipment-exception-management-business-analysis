Shipment exceptions, handled before the customer calls

How a fictional operator could tell customers about delays before they call — framed, specified, built as a working demo, accepted and brought live in a controlled pilot.

Independent portfolio case. Tidewell Logistics is fictional; workshops, sprints and acceptance tests are simulated; figures are illustrative. Labels: proposed business case and plans · simulated fictional delivery · measured automated tests run in the repository.

The problem

22%
of late deliveries announced before the window
38%
of all contacts are “where is my load?”
±55 / wk
missed delivery slots at ±€140
3 → 1
three requested solutions, one root cause: no rules for late and no owner

The recommendation proposed

A small exception component next to the TMS: rules decide severity, owner and response time; planners work from one list; customers get one clear message. Scored 3.95 against 2.45–3.10 for the alternatives on weights agreed beforehand.

TMS change→13 rules→owner + 30 min / 2 h→one message→resolved with reason
Buy the information first Payback ±13.5 months if a third of WISMO calls disappear (pessimistic: ±31 months). So: a two-sprint pilot for five customers with a control group, ±€114k; release 2 only if WISMO falls ≥ 20%.

Limits

No real stakeholders, data, TMS or deployment; architecture and privacy controls are proposals; no usability test with real people yet. The case shows how the analyst frames, decides, specifies and prepares delivery — it is not a substitute for team experience.
