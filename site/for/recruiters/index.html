The case in five minutes

A business analysis portfolio case for a fictional short-sea and door-to-door logistics operator. Five steps, each with one thing to open. No installation needed.

  1. 1 The problem 30 seconds

    Customers learned about delivery delays when they arrived at the gate. Three managers asked for three different solutions. The analysis showed the real gap was missing rules: what counts as late, how serious it is, who acts, and who tells the customer.

    Problem statement · one-page summary

  2. 2 What the analyst did 1 minute

    Framed four options with a business case and named its riskiest assumption → ran discovery and an event storming → modelled AS-IS/TO-BE in BPMN 2.0 → wrote 13 rules with 30 examples that run as tests → prioritised the backlog with the Product Owner (WSJF) → designed the screens → organised Business Acceptance Testing → prepared go/no-go, rollback, support handover and measurement.

    The journey · case study · executive case study (PDF)

  3. 3 See it work 2 minutes

    Open the control tower and follow the guided scenario on the right: a reefer for a key account is on schedule, its sailing is delayed six hours, the rules classify it as critical, the shift lead assigns it, the planner acts, the customer sees one clear message, a new window is agreed, and the audit trail and the test results show why.

  4. 4 The evidence — and what is not claimed 1 minute

    Each typical BA responsibility is mapped to an artefact, and each line says what is not claimed: there were no real stakeholders, no real sprints and no real BAT. The automated tests are real and run on every change.

    Evidence matrix · test results · by responsibility

  5. 5 Can the author defend it? 30 seconds

    The repository includes a Dutch learning lab with ten modules and a set of competency tasks. It says openly that the case was built with AI assistance and that competence is shown by the author's own exercises, not by the files.
