The scenario in 65 seconds

The challenge

Since the new TMS went live, customers see statuses — but not whether their delivery window is at risk. So they call.

  • Customers hear it at the gateOnly 22% of late deliveries were announced before the window started.
  • Customer service as a switchboard±1,250 “where is my load?” contacts a week — 38% of all contacts.
  • Nobody owns the exceptionPlanners notice delays by chance and copy orders into Excel.

My role

Business analyst in a Scrum team: problem framing, discovery and workshops, process and rule design, backlog preparation with the Product Owner, UX, acceptance and production readiness.

  • AnalysisProblem framing, options and business case, discovery, event storming, registers.

A pain point stays traceable through requirement, rule, story, test and KPI — and a script checks it on every change.

01

Detect and classify

Only what puts the promise at risk becomes an exception.

Problem

Every status change looked the same, so people filtered by gut feeling; three teams counted “late” differently.

Design choice

Late = after the end of the agreed window (D-01). Severity from minutes late; reefer one level up; cancellations always critical.

TMS change→Evaluate rules→Severity→Owner + response time

02

Own and resolve

One list, one owner, one clock.

Problem

Delays were noticed by chance; nobody was accountable for acting within a time.

Design choice

Owner group by cause; 30 min / 2 h response time as BPMN timer events; escalation to the shift lead; mandatory reason code.

Workbench→Acknowledge→Re-plan window→Resolve with reason

TO-BE BPMN ↗ · State machine ↗ · User stories ↗

03

Tell the customer first

One clear message, not a message storm.

Problem

Only key accounts whose account manager happened to know were told; standard TMS e-mails would be noise.

Design choice

Notify major/critical, hold missing status until confirmed, throttle per order, recovery message only if told before, call key accounts.

Decision→Planner note (5 min)→Portal + e-mail→Back on schedule

04

Prove it before scaling

Buy the information first.

Problem

The business case stands or falls with one unknown: how many “where is my load?” calls disappear.

Design choice

Two-sprint pilot for five customers with a control group in shadow mode; fund release 2 only at ≥ 20% fewer WISMO contacts.

Pilot→Daily export→Pilot vs. control→Stage gate

Business and IT, connected

A small component next to the TMS — not a new platform. Behaviour is specified precisely enough to build and test; the demo proves it.

Proposed architecture (release 1)

TMS (SaaS)→Signed webhook→Exception component→Workbench + notification→Customer portal / e-mail

Limitations

  • Fictional organisation; interviews, workshops, sprints, BAT and the pilot readout are simulated.
  • No real TMS, e-mail or load testing; the demo runs in the browser on a mock API.
  • Architecture, privacy and runbook are proposals, not reviewed by a real architect, DPO or support team.
  • No usability test with real participants yet.

Lessons

  • Rules before screens: the gap was a missing definition, not a missing tool.
  • Passing stories are not a met need — three of four BAT majors came from real readers.
  • Building is a review: the demo exposed five open questions and a privacy leak.
  • Answer customer-facing questions with the PO, not alone (DEF-04).
