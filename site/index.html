The challenge

Tidewell Logistics (fictional) moves trailers door to door between the Continent, the UK and Ireland. Its new transport management system shows customers statuses — but not whether their delivery window is at risk. So they call.

My role in this case

Business analyst in the Scrum team Customer Visibility, next to the Product Owner. Three people asked for three solutions — a tracking page, mails to key accounts, "nothing that adds work". I:

  • framed the problem and compared four options in a business case with its riskiest assumption named;
  • ran discovery and a big-picture event storming, and turned six hotspots into decisions by their owners;
  • modelled the AS-IS and TO-BE in BPMN 2.0 and the release in UML;
  • wrote 13 exception rules and 30 decision examples that run as tests;
  • prepared the backlog with the PO — story map, WSJF prioritisation, a two-sprint pilot;
  • designed the planner workbench and the customer message, in Figma and as a clickable prototype;
  • organised Business Acceptance Testing with key users, prepared the go/no-go, the communication and the support handover.

Is it worth it? Four options, one honest business case

"Switch on the standard TMS e-mails" was cheap and fast. I tested it on paper with a week of real-looking status events: ±9 messages per order, 0.3 of which mattered. Two pilot customers said they would filter them out. More messages is not better information.

The recommended option pays back in ±13.5 months — if a third of the "where is my load?" contacts disappear. Nobody knew that number, so the sponsor funded a six-week pilot with a stage gate instead of the whole thing.

Rules before screens

The root cause was not a missing screen but missing rules: what counts as late, how bad it is, who acts, who is told. The event storming made that visible; severity turned "tell key accounts everything" and "no notification storm" from opposites into one table.

  • Late = after the end of the agreed window, not after the planned ETA
  • Minor < 2 h · major 2–6 h · critical ≥ 6 h or next day · reefer one level up
  • Missing status: the planner confirms before the customer is alarmed
  • At most one message per order every 2 h — unless it got worse
